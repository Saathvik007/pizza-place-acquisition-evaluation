# Pizza Place Project: valuing a pizzeria purchase

Author: Saathvik Maheshuni

This was my term project for Financial Analysis for Tech Managers at NYU Tandon in spring 2026. It has five deliverables that build on each other. I started by visiting a real pizzeria near campus. Then I used the course's Excel templates to plan the purchase of a delivery scooter, and at the end I valued buying a whole pizzeria and worked out the best way to pay for it.

## What I found

The main question (Deliverable 3) was whether to buy Sagnotti's Pizzeria for $300,000 and how to pay for it. I built five years of pro forma statements from a $1,000,000 revenue base growing 3% a year, with food costs at 30% of sales. Free cash flow in the first year after the purchase (Year 4) comes to $133,500.

I compared two ways to pay:

| | Option A: all cash | Option B: $100,000 cash and a $200,000 loan at 7% |
|---|---|---|
| Discount rate (WACC) | 9.00% | 6.50% |
| NPV | $248,906 | $487,258 |
| IRR | 36.71% | 134.34% |
| Payback | 2.20 years | 0.75 years |

Using CAPM with a 4.00% risk-free rate, a beta of 0.85 and a 6.00% market risk premium, the minimum return to accept is 9.10%. Both options beat it, and I recommended Option B. Loan interest is tax-deductible at a 25% rate, so the after-tax cost of debt is 5.25%, and the owner keeps $200,000 of their own cash.

The earlier deliverables:

- D1: I visited Artichoke Basille's Pizza on MacDougal Street and talked to an employee. I used what I saw to explain agency theory (the expensive sauces are made at another location and kept in a locked cooler) and operating leverage (high fixed rent, $6.95 slices and a lot of late-night sales).
- D2: I compared three ways to pay for a $4,500 electric delivery scooter: cash, a 5-year loan at 7% ($89.11 a month) or a lease at $100 a month ($6,000 over five years). I picked the loan, and debt to assets peaks at 0.2778 in Year 4. Saving $117.64 a month at 5% builds the $8,000 needed for a replacement scooter in five years.
- D4: an 11-slide final presentation of the whole project.
- D5: my written reflection on what I learned.

## Notes and limitations

- The statements started from the course's Excel templates, which had made-up numbers. I changed them using what I saw at the pizzeria and public prices, so they are my estimates, not the pizzeria's real books. The $1,000,000 revenue base in D3 came from the project instructions.
- Option B discounts the business's cash flows before loan payments but only subtracts the $100,000 of cash. That makes its NPV and IRR look higher than they would if the loan payments were taken out first, so I treat Option B's numbers as an upper bound.
- After the course I corrected the NPV and IRR figures in the D3 report, the deck and the reflection so they match the D3 workbook. The versions I turned in quoted slightly different numbers.
- The video of my verbal presentation isn't included.
